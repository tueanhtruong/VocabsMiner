# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Pets and Children

**Đề bài:** While many families are keeping pets at home, some people fear that pets can harm children. What threats can pets cause to children? How to minimize those threats?

---

## 1. Phân tích đề

- Dạng bài: **Two-part question** (không phải discussion/opinion) — cần trả lời rõ 2 câu hỏi:
  1. Threats gì? (nguyên nhân/vấn đề)
  2. Cách giảm thiểu? (giải pháp)
- Cấu trúc gợi ý: Intro → Body 1 (threats) → Body 2 (solutions) → Conclusion
- Không cần nêu ý kiến cá nhân (opinion) vì đề không hỏi "do you agree" — chỉ cần trình bày khách quan.

## 2. Gợi ý dàn ý (Brainstorm)

**Threats (Body 1):**

- Physical harm: bites, scratches — especially from large or untrained dogs; unpredictable behaviour when provoked
- Health/hygiene: zoonotic diseases (rabies, ringworm, parasites), allergies, asthma triggered by pet dander
- Psychological: young children may develop fear/phobia after a bad encounter
- Accidental harm: child pulling tail/ears → pet reacts defensively; tripping over pets

**Solutions (Body 2):**

- Parental supervision: never leave young children alone with pets, especially unfamiliar ones
- Education: teach children how to approach/handle animals gently, read animal body language
- Training & vaccination: proper obedience training for pets; regular vet check-ups and vaccinations
- Breed/species selection: choose child-friendly, calm breeds for families with young kids
- Hygiene practices: hand-washing after contact, regular grooming/deworming of pets

## 3. Band-differentiated sample essays

### 🟡 Band 5.5–6.0 (Simple, more common errors, basic linking)

Nowadays, a lot of families like to keep pets such as dogs or cats in their house. However, some people worry that pets can be dangerous for children. This essay will talk about the threats and how to reduce them.

Firstly, one of the biggest threat is that pets can bite or scratch children, especially if the animal is big or not trained well. Small kids often don't know how to play with animals properly, so they may pull the pet's tail or ears, and the pet will react by biting them. This can cause serious injury. Secondly, pets can also carry diseases like rabies or skin infections, which can pass to children if they touch or get bitten by the animal. In addition, some children are allergic to animal fur, so keeping a pet at home might make them sick with breathing problems.

To reduce these threats, there are several things parents can do. The most important solution is that parents should always supervise their children when they are around pets, especially when the child is very young. Parents should never leave a small child alone with an animal. Another solution is to teach children how to treat animals gently and not to disturb them while they are eating or sleeping. Also, it is very important for pet owners to take their animals to the vet regularly for vaccination, this can prevent diseases from spreading to humans. Finally, families should choose pets that are known to be gentle and good with children, rather than aggressive breeds.

In conclusion, although pets can bring some risks to children such as bites, disease and allergy, these problems can be reduced if parents supervise carefully and take proper care of their pets.

_(~280 words)_

### 🟢 Band 6.5–7.0 (Clearer structure, better vocabulary range, fewer errors)

Pet ownership has become increasingly common among modern households, yet growing concerns exist regarding the potential dangers pets may pose to young children. This essay will examine the main risks involved and outline practical measures to minimise them.

Several threats can arise when children interact with domestic animals. The most obvious is physical injury, as bites and scratches can occur when a pet feels threatened or provoked, particularly by a curious toddler who does not understand appropriate boundaries. Larger or poorly trained dogs pose a greater risk in this respect. Beyond physical harm, pets can also transmit zoonotic diseases such as ringworm or, in rare cases, rabies, through direct contact or bites. Furthermore, allergic reactions triggered by pet dander are a common issue, sometimes leading to respiratory problems like asthma in sensitive children. Finally, a frightening encounter with an animal can leave a lasting psychological impact, resulting in a long-term fear of animals.

Fortunately, these risks can be substantially reduced through a combination of supervision and education. Parents bear the primary responsibility of never leaving infants or toddlers unsupervised with pets, regardless of how docile the animal appears to be. Equally important is teaching children, from an early age, how to approach and handle animals respectfully, such as avoiding sudden movements or disturbing a pet while it is eating. On the owner's side, ensuring pets receive regular veterinary care, including vaccinations and deworming, greatly lowers the likelihood of disease transmission. Selecting breeds with a reputation for gentle temperaments is also advisable for households with young children.

In conclusion, while pets undeniably carry certain risks—ranging from physical injury to disease and allergies—these dangers are largely manageable. Through consistent parental supervision, proper pet care, and early education, families can enjoy the companionship of pets while keeping their children safe.

_(~305 words)_

### 🔵 Band 8.0+ (Sophisticated language, precise collocations, cohesive complex sentences)

As pet ownership continues to rise across households worldwide, so too does public unease about the hazards animals might pose to young family members. This essay explores the principal dangers children face when living with pets and considers effective strategies for mitigating them.

Domestic animals can jeopardise children's wellbeing in several distinct ways. Perhaps the most immediate concern is physical injury: a startled or provoked animal, particularly a large dog, may bite or scratch, inflicting wounds that range from minor to severe. Toddlers, lacking the awareness to interpret an animal's warning signals, are especially vulnerable to such incidents. A second, less visible threat lies in the transmission of zoonotic illnesses—conditions such as ringworm, toxoplasmosis, or, in unvaccinated animals, rabies—which can be contracted through bites, scratches, or even casual contact with fur and saliva. Allergic sensitivities constitute a further risk, as prolonged exposure to pet dander can precipitate chronic respiratory conditions, including asthma, in predisposed children. Beyond the physical, a single traumatic encounter with an aggressive animal can instil a lasting cynophobia or general fear of animals that persists into adulthood.

Nevertheless, these risks are far from insurmountable, provided households adopt a proactive approach. Constant parental vigilance remains paramount; young children should never be left unattended in the presence of an animal, irrespective of its perceived temperament. Complementing this, early education on animal behaviour—teaching children to recognise signs of distress and to interact gently rather than provocatively—can pre-empt many defensive reactions. On the animal's side, routine veterinary care, encompassing vaccinations and parasite control, substantially curtails the risk of disease transmission, while professional obedience training instils predictable, non-aggressive behaviour. Families with very young children might also consider selecting breeds renowned for their placid temperament, thereby reducing risk from the outset.

In conclusion, although pets can expose children to genuine hazards spanning physical injury, disease, and psychological trauma, these dangers can be considerably curtailed through vigilant supervision, deliberate education, and responsible pet ownership. When these precautions are conscientiously observed, the benefits of pet companionship can be enjoyed without undue risk to children's safety.

_(~320 words)_
